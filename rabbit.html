Quick viva questions

1. Why is one eye used as a control?

To compare against the test eye and rule out effects of handling or drops. Saline is inert.

2. Atropine: pupil and light reflex?

Mydriasis, light reflex absent. It blocks muscarinic receptors, so the sphincter pupillae is paralysed.

3. How do you tell ephedrine from atropine?

Ephedrine gives mydriasis with light reflex present. Atropine abolishes the reflex.

4. Mechanism of ephedrine mydriasis?

Mainly indirect: releases noradrenaline, which acts on α1 receptors to contract the radial dilator.

5. Physostigmine effect on the eye?

Miosis and lower IOP. It inhibits cholinesterase, so acetylcholine builds up and contracts the sphincter and ciliary muscle.

6. Lignocaine effect, and why?

Corneal reflex lost. It blocks Na⁺ channels in corneal sensory nerves. The pupil is unchanged.

7. Nerves of the light reflex?

Afferent: optic nerve (II). Efferent: oculomotor nerve (III), parasympathetic.

8. Nerves of the corneal reflex?

Afferent: trigeminal (V1). Efferent: facial (VII), which closes the lid.

9. Which mydriatic is dangerous in narrow-angle glaucoma?

Atropine and other mydriatics. A dilated pupil crowds the angle and blocks aqueous outflow.

10. What is cycloplegia?

Paralysis of accommodation from ciliary muscle block. Atropine causes it and lasts about 7 to 10 days.

11. Adrenaline effect on IOP?

Lowers it by reducing aqueous production and increasing uveoscleral outflow. It also dilates the pupil.

12. Uses of physostigmine (miotic) in the eye?

Open-angle glaucoma, and reversing the effect of mydriatics.
