Model scope. Relative teaching responses reproduce the supplied worksheet patterns. They are not patient dosing guidance or a quantitative physiological model.

How to run the simulator

  1. Select one of the seven experiments.
  2. Choose a drug and dose. The predicted response updates before you add it.
  3. Select Add to tissue or Administer IV. Each addition is appended to the trace.
  4. Add blockers before agonists where required. Tissue state persists until reset.
  5. Use Run standard protocol to reproduce the worksheet order automatically.
  6. Turn on Expected trace to compare your sequence with the reference pattern.

Important sequence note

For Experiment 2, the first ACh response immediately after physostigmine is potentiated (about 2.5×). The next ACh response is partly recovered, resolving the worksheet caption conflict as specified in the written reference.

Reading the graphs

Frog rectus contractures rise upward. Dog blood-pressure depressor responses fall below baseline; pressor responses rise above it. All amplitudes are relative teaching units.

Pharmacology glossary

Potentiation

A drug with little or no effect by itself enhances another drug’s response. Physostigmine potentiates ACh by inhibiting its hydrolysis.

Summation

The combined effect equals the sum of the individual drug effects.

Synergism

A general term for a combined effect greater than expected from the individual actions; potentiation is one form.

EC50 / ED50

The concentration or dose producing 50% of the maximal response; an index of potency.

Emax

The maximum response a drug can produce; an index of efficacy.

Competitive antagonism

A reversible antagonist competes with the agonist for the same receptor, causing a parallel rightward dose–response shift. Enough agonist can still achieve Emax.

Non-competitive antagonism

The antagonist reduces the agonist’s effectiveness without simple surmountable competition; Emax is reduced.

Tachyphylaxis

Rapid reduction in response after repeated doses at short intervals, as with tyramine-induced depletion of releasable noradrenaline.

Tolerance

A reduced response that generally develops more gradually with repeated exposure, for example with nitrates or opioids.
